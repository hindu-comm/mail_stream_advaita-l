>> Shankara, in his prasthanatraya bhashyas, has explicitly advocated the
>> physical puja of the Guru:
>>
>> We find the following three verses at the beginning of the Taittiriya
>> Bhashyam, one of which is a direct namaskara to his Purvacharyas who have
>> expounded the Upanishads:
>>
>> यैरिमे गुरुभिः पूर्वं पदवाक्यप्रमाणतः । व्याख्याताः
>> सर्ववेदान्तास्तान्नित्यं प्रणतोऽस्म्यहम् ॥ २ ॥
>> At the end of the Mandukya bhashya he composes verses in veneration of
>> his Guru and Paramaguru.
>> In the Mundaka bhashya explaining the mantra which says: the aspirants
>> for worldly benefits and moksha have to venerate the Brahma Jnani, Shankara
>> says: by prakakshina namaskara,etc.
>> At the end of the Prashnopanishad bhashya, while the aspirants are paying
>> their respects to their Guru Pippalada for having given them the
>> Brahmavidya, the upanishad says: tam archantaH, Shankara says: by
>> submitting flowers at the feet of the Guru, etc.
>> So, Shankara is quite open to the Puja of the Acharya.
>> Interestingly we find similar, almost same, wordings in Shankara's
>> opening and closing verses for the 17th chapter of the Upadeshasahasri.
>> regards
>> subbu
