> Please give an English substance of this:
>
> An article titled 'The Vision of the Supreme Being' appeared in the
> October 2025 issue of "Adhyatma Prakash" monthly magazine.
> Author: Sri Sri Sachidanandendra Saraswati Swami, Adhyatma Prakash
> Karyalaya, Holenarasipur / Bangalore.
>
> Is it possible to see the Supreme Lord in His true form? It is described
> in the Puranas that devotees saw Him with their own eyes in the form of
> their favorite deity and received His grace. But that form was a temporary
> form that the Lord assumed to bless the devotees. Similarly, when
> unrighteousness increased and religion declined, it is written in the
> histories that the Lord incarnated in forms like Ramakrishna for the
> upliftment of religion; that too was a temporary form that the Lord assumed
> from Maya for the benefit of the world. Is it possible to see the Supreme
> Lord in the form that He has always existed? The Upanishads answer this
> question as follows:
>
> I have seen the house of the Lord, and I have heard the words
> M. 3-1-8
>
> The eternal form of the Supreme Being has no shape or color. Therefore, He
> cannot be seen with the eyes. He does not have any special features such as
> caste, substance, qualities, action, relationship; therefore, it is not
> possible to express Him by any word. Since there is no sound, touch, form,
> taste, or smell, He cannot be known by the senses of touch, skin, eyes,
> tongue, or smell; for this reason, He cannot be contemplated by the mind.
> There is a scripture that even the heavens can be attained by the actions
> mentioned in the Vedas, such as the Agnihotra. But
> The Supreme Being's supreme form cannot be attained by any action. Because
> He is not something separate from the aspirant and far away to be attained.
> It is well-known in the scriptures that many great fruits are attained
> through penance. But the Supreme Being, who is always ready, cannot be
> attained through penance. It is not that the sacred actions of sacrifices
> and penances have no fruit; it is just that it is never possible to attain
> the Supreme Being directly through them.
>
> So what is the means of seeing the Supreme Soul that is different from all
> these? Someone may ask. The Supreme Soul is the true All-Soul that is deep
> within all of us. He is different from all the false souls that we
> mistakenly think of as 'I', 'me', and because He pervades all of them, He
> is called 'the Supreme Soul'. It is through the experience of that Supreme
> Soul, which is the form of consciousness, that we come to know the mind and
> the senses. It is through His influence that the mind and the senses
> illuminate and show their respective things.
>
> Our mind has become polluted by the desires and hatred of external things;
> that is why, although it has the power to understand the things related to
> it, it does not have the power to understand even the Supreme Being who is
> very close. Just as if a mirror is cleaned, or if a still body of water is
> brought to a standstill, the reflection of the rising sun falls on it,
> similarly, if the mind is withdrawn from external things and directed
> inward, and if one concentrates and makes an effort to see the Self, the
> image of that Supreme Being falls on the mind. Then the mind gives up its
> own mind and becomes immersed in the Supreme Being and becomes the Supreme
> Being. Through this meditation, the Supreme Being can be found.
>
> The vision of the Supreme Soul is not to know Him by objectifying Him
> through any object; how, and by whom, can the all-pervading, immaterial,
> omnipresent, omniscient, be known? When the mind is concentrated and
> meditated upon, it takes on the form of the Supreme Soul; the unique
> experience that is then attained is the vision of the Self.

>> Here’s a *concise bullet-point version* of the article’s substance:
>>
>>    -
>>
>>    *Question:* Can Paramātman be seen in His true form?
>>    -
>>
>>    *Purāṇic accounts:* Devotees see chosen deity-forms; avatāras like
>>    Rāma, Kṛṣṇa are assumed for the world’s welfare. These are temporary, māyic
>>    forms.
>>    -
>>
>>    *Upaniṣadic teaching (Muṇḍaka 3.1.8):*
>>    -
>>
>>       Paramātman has no form, color, or qualities.
>>       -
>>
>>       He cannot be grasped by eyes, speech, mind, senses, ritual, or
>>       austerity.
>>       -
>>
>>    *Reason:* He is not an external object to be acquired; He is
>>    ever-present, the innermost Self.
>>    -
>>
>>    *Nature of Paramātman:* The universal Self, the consciousness that
>>    illumines mind and senses.
>>    -
>>
>>    *Obstacle:* The mind is impure, restless, turned outward by likes and
>>    dislikes, so it cannot reveal Him.
>>    -
>>
>>    *Method:*
>>    -
>>
>>       Withdraw the mind from externals, purify and steady it.
>>       -
>>
>>       In deep concentration, the mind reflects Paramātman like a clean
>>       mirror or calm water reflects light.
>>       -
>>
>>       The mind then loses its individuality and merges in Paramātman.
>>       -
>>
>>    *Result:*
>>    -
>>
>>       Paramātman is not objectified but realized directly.
>>       -
>>
>>       This unique inner experience is true *Ātma-darśana* (vision of the
>>       Self).
